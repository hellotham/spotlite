July 2004, comparing four Windows programs for watching and recording digital television in
Australia. It is reproduced from the Word manuscript, which is the file as it left my hands
rather than the magazine's page._

what was delivered._

All of these are mine from 2004 rather than damage in conversion, so they stand._

## Story Summary

APC reviews software capable of scheduling, recording and playing-back Digital TV in Australia.
We investigate which have the best HTPC menu systems for other multimedia applications like
picture slideshows and music playback, but also consider ease of set up and technical
reliability.

## Introduction

The market for Digital TV tuner cards is rapidly maturing the price of these cards are dropping
to the level where digital TV tuner cards are now cheaper than analogue cards with hardware MPEG2
encoding/decoding, and deliver much better picture quality as they capture MPEG2 digitally "off
the air" without analogue "artefacts" such as ghosting or noise.

Digital TV capable software are also maturing, but more slowly. Many manufacturers have
implemented proprietary drivers for their cards, and often buyers have no alternatives other than
the bundled software that came with the cards.

Microsoft has been promoting Broadcast Digital Architecture (BDA) as an emerging standard for TV
tuner card drivers. To date, few cards have BDA drivers (and some of these are user-contributed
rather than vendor-supported) because there is only one "application" that uses BDA: Microsoft's
Windows XP Media Center Edition (which is only sold with a pre-built PC). However, third party
BDA-aware applications are finally coming out.

In the open source/Linux community, the LinuxTV DVB project is now part of the "production" Linux
2.6.x release. It currently supports mainly the Technotrend DVB-T PCI card (sold in Australia
under the Hauppauge and DPANDA brand names), although more recently support has been added for
Nebula DigiTV, Avermedia DVB-T and a few other cards not sold in Australia.

In this article, APC examines currently available "third party" Digital TV capable software (ie.
software not bundled with the hardware). All software have been tested with the TwinHan
VisionPlus DVB cards, using either the manufacturer supplied driver or the user-contributed BDA
driver.

The ability to support downloadable Electronic Program Guides (EPG) is an issue, and none of the
software featured will support Australian EPG downloads "out of the box." There are no
"official"/supported downloadable source of EPG data in Australia, unlike in the US and Europe
where there are a number of such providers. Although Digital TV broadcasts contain EPG
information in the broadcast EIT (extra information table) embedded in the transport stream, it's
limited to "Now" and "Next" programme information. The other option is download a utility such as
`tv_grab_au`, TVHarvest, NebulaEPG, etc. These utilities convert information from public websites
into a format (such as XMLTV) that can be used by the Digital TV software. Hopefully, the
situation will improve in the near future.

Want the "ultimate home media centre?"

Showshifter supports not just the usual DVR features like pausing live TV and time-shift
recordings, but also works as a DVD/multimedia player, picture viewer, music jukebox for a
complete turnkey solution. It even supports operation over a network and can be operated using a
supported remote control.

Earlier versions of Showshifter only supported analogue TV tuner cards, but there is now a new
version called Showshifter DVB, with support limited to the Technotrend/Hauppauge/DPANDA DVB-T
PCI/USB tuners. (It has support for Nebula DigiTV, but "not Australia.") There is a beta version
(3.02.2595) of Showshifter DVB that is BDA-aware, so this was the version tested using the
Spectrum BDA drivers for the VisionPlus card.

EPG support is enabled through a utility called HMNXMLTV that can import XMLTV listings into its
internal EPG called "ShowGuide." It is extensible through plug-in modules and the user interface
is skinnable.

The installation process was very user friendly and straightforward, and the channel tuning
process found all available digital TV channels.

Showshifter has the most visually attractive and easy to use interface, with all functions in
large font and easy to read at TV resolutions. The beta version appeared to have a few bugs and
the application crashed at least once.

_myHTPC: a very nice-looking front end with integrated EPG and TV scheduling capabilities_

Want an attractive front-end for your HTPC that supports an Australian EPG and point-and-click
scheduling of TV programmes?

myHTPC (current version Alpha Release 24) is strictly speaking not a Digital TV application, but
it is Digital TV "aware" through "plug-ins." myHTPC is actually a customisable, menu-driven HTPC
front end that, according to the author, allows you to "to launch external programs, browse
pictures and large collections of almost any type of media stored on your computer."

The DABDig plug-in integrates myHTPC to a Digital TV application of your choice, such as the
bundled software that comes with your card through a freeware program called DABDig (current
version 2.50b14). DABDig supports a large number of both analog and digital TV tuner cards,
including the TwinHan VisionPlus, Nebula DigiTV, Hauppauge Nova-T, DPANDA DVB-T and the AVerMedia
AVerTV DVB-T.

myHTPC with the DABDig plug-in has an EPG (sourced from XMLTV listing files) to allow you to
browse TV listings. It will also allow you to schedule TV recordings (including recurring
recordings).

The author of myHTPC is currently working on releasing a commercial version called Meedio
Essentials, that hopefully will be released by the time you read this. There will also be version
called Meedio TV that supports Live TV and DVR capabilities.

Integrating myHTPC with DABDig and your favourite DVR application is fairly complex (see the APC
tutorial on page XX) but the software appears to work seamlessly and the user interface is very
attractive and optimized for TV resolutions.

_DigitalWatch: a minimalist user interface that is primarily oriented for watching TV_

Tired of cluttered and complicated user interfaces and just want a minimalist application for
watching TV?

DigitalWatch is a free, open-source, Digital TV viewing and recording application. The current
version (0.701) only supports the TwinHan VisionPlus PCI card, but there is a BDA version (0.722)
in beta.

The author says that DigitalWatch is "oriented towards watching rather than recording although it
does have recording capabilities" and recommends another tool called DVB TV Web Scheduler (BDA
version also in development) for timed recordings.

The installation process is fairly manual and very few instructions are provided. TV channel
scanning is done using an external utility and then manually entered into the `channels.ini`
configuration file. The web site says channel configuration files for "most capital cities" are
supplied, but we did not find them in the installation package.

The biggest drawcard for DigitalWatch is probably the ability to pick and choose the audio and
video decoders of your choice, which allows tinkerers to experiment to see which ones have the
best quality and stability. It also supports a screen zoom feature, which is useful for watching
widescreen programs on a 4x3 display, or watching 4x3 programs on a widescreen display.

Some of the features such as Now and Next and channel strength/quality indicator did not work on
the version tested.

The user interface is designed to appeal to users who want to watch TV without any screen
clutter. Moving the mouse to the bottom of the frame will bring up a menu bar, but the software
is designed to be best used through the keyboard or remote control.

Want a free but functional alternative to the bundled application that came with your tuner card?

The current version of ProgDVB (4.40.2 test) supports the TwinHan VisionPlus and
Technotrend/Hauppauge/DPANDA cards, plus a number of other cards not sold in Australia. It
supports both watching and recording Digital TV, and is extensible through user-contributed
plug-ins.

Installation was relatively easy, but the TV channel scanning function (Channel List | Channel
Search | Search All Transponders) did not find any channels. However, if a scan is performed
individually for each TV station frequency, the program managed to detect and lock in to all
channels successfully. In addition, the software allows users to pick and choose various decoding
filter configurations, which allows tinkerers to experiment to see which ones have the best
quality and stability.

The EPG feature is limited to Now and Next information. There is an additional EPG plug-in
available (EPG-plugin from Relict Marauder) but this does not appear to support XMLTV. ProgDVB
appears to support multiple recording formats, but recommends PVA ("Packetized Audio and Video
PES").

The Teletext feature appears to work, but subtitles didn't. ProgDVB has a nice balance between
features and usability. Even though the software is still under development, it shows a lot of
promise and is worth considering as an alternative to the bundled application that came with your
tuner card.

## Boxout: What to look for

If you are looking at Digital TV software, you may want to consider the following:

**EPG support** – Does the software support Now and Next information? Does it support
downloadable EPG listings for Australia? Can it import XMLTV format EPG listings?

**Extensibility** – Does it support plug-ins? What plug-ins are currently available? What other
functionality does it support besides watching and recording TV?

**Hardware support** – Is the software supported on your card? If it isn't, time to look
elsewhere, or ask the company/author if they are planning support in the near future.

**Installation** – How easy/automated is the software installation process? Are the instructions
easy to follow? Is the configuration done through a menu/wizard or do you need to edit
configuration files?

**HDTV** – Does it support HDTV channels or only SDTV channels? Does it support Dolby Digital
(AC3) audio streams?

**Recording format** – Does it support HDTV recordings? What format does it record to (PVA,
transport stream, program stream) and can the format be converted to program stream for DVD
burning? Does it have DVD burning capabilities?

**Timeshifting** – Can you pause Live TV? Can you schedule recordings? Using an EPG? Can it
schedule recurring/repeating recordings? Is there an external/remote interface (API or web) for
scheduling recordings?

**TV channel scanning** – Did the software tune Australian channels easily? Are there any
pre-configured channel files for your location?

**Usability** – Is the software easy to use? Does it support a remote control? Does the user
interface get in the way when you just want to watch TV? Is the user interface readable at TV
resolutions?
